# Student instructions: Pinpoint document-mining exercise

## Your role

You are helping a reporter quickly review a collection of public documents from the Prince George's County Board of License Commissioners.

Your job is to use Pinpoint to find one possible story lead, identify the source material behind it, and decide how much verification is still needed before the lead could be used in a story pitch.

## Duration

About 20 minutes.

## Before you start

Open the Pinpoint collection and notice the main parts of the interface:

- The **Documents** list
- The search bar
- The **Gemini** panel on the right
- The source setting, such as **Entire collection**
- The Gemini options: **Find answers**, **Summarize**, **Compare**, **Extract data**, and **Label documents**
- The warning about inaccurate information.

## Step 1: Orient to the collection

Before searching or asking Gemini anything, look at the collection as a whole. Let's discuss:

- What documents are in this collection?
- Who created them?
- What time period do they cover?
- What kinds of questions could these documents help answer?
- What would these documents not be able to answer?

## Step 2: Use manual search first

Use the search bar to search for an establishment, organization, place, issue, rule number, license type, or hearing type.

Possible search terms:

- `violation`
- `transfer`
- `license`
- `beer`
- `wine`
- `Hyattsville`
- `College Park`
- `preliminary matters`

Questions:
1) Which search terms did you use?
2) Are there any interesting documents that mention the thing you searched?
3) Is the mention central or incidental?
4) Think about the risk level of this task. Search is mostly about finding source material, but you still need to read the document and inspect the context. What are your thoughts about you just did, in terms of risks and rewards?


## Step 3: Use Gemini to summarize cautiously

Use **Summarize** on one document or severeal selected documents.

Questions:
1) What did the summary capture accurately?
2) What did it omit?
3) Did it overstate anything?
4) What exact source passage would you check before using the summary?


## Step 4: Use Gemini to extract data

Use **Extract data** to pull structured information from the documents.

Try a prompt like:

> Extract a table with these columns: source document, hearing date, establishment name, address, matter type, license type, alleged violation, disposition or action, and notes. If a field is not available, write "not found."

Questions:

1) Was the output clear?
2) Were there problematic fields?
3) Did it include source documents or passages?
4) What would happen if an extraction error were repeated across many rows?


## Step 5: Use Gemini to find answers

Use **Find answers** to ask one collection-level question.

Possible questions:

- What types of matters appear in these documents?
- Which establishments appear more than once?
- What kinds of alleged violations are mentioned?
- Which documents mention transfers or new licenses?

Questions:

1) What questions did you ask?
2) Was Pinpoint effective in giving the answers?
3) Did the answer appear complete?
4) Again, think about the risk level of this task. What are your thoughts about you just did, in terms of risks and rewards?


## Step 6: Write one possible story angle

Use what you found to write one possible story angle. You may phrase it as a question or a hypothesis. Then list:

- What evidence you have.
- What evidence you do not have.
- Who you would contact next.
- What data or documents you would need next.
