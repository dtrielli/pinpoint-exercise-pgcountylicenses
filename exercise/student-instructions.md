# Student instructions

## Your role

You are helping a reporter quickly review a collection of public documents.

Your job is to find one possible story lead, identify the source material behind it, and decide how much verification is still needed.

## Time

Plan for about 20 minutes.

## Step 1: Orient to the collection

Before searching, look at the collection as a whole.

Answer:

- What documents are in this collection?
- Who created them?
- What time period do they cover?
- What kinds of questions could these documents help answer?
- What would these documents not be able to answer?

## Step 2: Search for a known term

Search for an establishment, organization, place, issue, rule number, or hearing type.

Record:

- The search term you used.
- Which documents mention it.
- Whether the mention is central or incidental.
- The exact passage or page you would save.

Think about the risk level of this task. Search is mostly about finding source material, but you still need to read the document.

## Step 3: Look for recurring entities

Use Pinpoint's people, organizations, or locations tools if available. If not, use repeated searches.

Record:

- A person, organization, or place that appears more than once.
- Any duplicated names or spelling variations.
- Anything that might be missing or misidentified.

Think about the risk level of this task. Entity extraction helps navigation, but it can miss names, split names, or combine different entities.

## Step 4: Summarize one document cautiously

Choose one document and write a cautious one- or two-sentence summary.

Use wording such as:

> This document appears to concern...

Then answer:

- What did you verify from the document itself?
- What did you infer?
- What would need more reporting?

Think about the risk level of this task. Summaries are useful for triage, but they can omit context or overstate what the document says.

## Step 5: Extract structured information

Choose a document with repeated fields, such as dates, establishments, addresses, rule numbers, allegations, dispositions, or hearing types.

Fill out the structured extraction table in the worksheet.

Think about the risk level of this task. Once extracted information becomes data, mistakes can spread into analysis.

## Step 6: Identify a claim

Find one sentence that sounds like a factual claim.

Answer:

- What is the claim?
- Who is making the claim?
- What document supports that the claim was made?
- Does the document prove the claim is true?
- What source would verify it?

Think about the risk level of this task. A document can prove that someone said something. It may not prove that the thing is true.

## Step 7: Write one possible story angle

Write the angle as a question, not a conclusion.

Examples:

- Why did...?
- How often...?
- Who benefits from...?
- What changed after...?
- What explains...?

Think about the risk level of this task. Story angles are leads for reporting, not findings.

## Final check

Before you finish, make sure your worksheet separates:

- What the document says.
- What the tool helped you find.
- What you inferred.
- What still needs verification.
